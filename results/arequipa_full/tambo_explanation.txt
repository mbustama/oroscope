HOW TO READ THESE NUMBERS
-------------------------
  Reported area is not physics-accepted area. The mask is closed
  morphologically with a 0.15 km element before areas are measured. Closing
  is not wrong — a site has to be a deployable region rather than a scatter
  of pixels — but it moves the number, and at Colca with a 1 km element it
  inflated the reported area by 2.29× against a stride-1 control. Set
  gap_close_km to 0 to see the accepted area directly.

  This run's own funnel puts that factor at 0.96×, so closing barely moved
  this result.

  Area and capacity are measured on different grids. Area comes from the map
  downsampled by 4, capacity from the full-resolution mask, so a feature a
  few pixels wide loses area while keeping its detectors — around 30% for a
  canyon strip. Use downsample_factor 1 for thin features.

  The scores rank sites against each other for one experiment and one energy
  band. They are not apertures, and no flux or detector response has been
  folded in.

  The layout is anchored, not fitted: detectors are placed from each site's
  bounding-box corner rather than optimised, so capacity is an estimate for
  an arbitrarily placed array.

WHICH OF THESE ARE ASSUMPTIONS
------------------------------
  Choices, not measurements. Check them before quoting a result.

  • min_score = 0.35
      A cut on a product of components, whose distribution piles up near
      zero, so any threshold in the middle sits on a cliff. Measured on one
      search: 0.0, 0.35 and 0.5 gave 45928, 2056 and zero detectors. This is
      the dominant assumption — prefer --score_percentile.
  • decay_spectral_index = 2, pinned
      The flux slope the decay term is folded against. Capacity varies by
      1.46× across a plausible range.
  • min_target_slope_deg = 25°
      The floor separating a canyon wall from a hillside. Strongly selective:
      0°, 25° and 35° gave 7442, 2056 and zero detectors.
  • max_range_km = unset
      Column depth accumulates over the whole profile walk, which then stops
      at max_dist_km — so the reported depth is a property of where the walk
      stopped, not of the target's thickness. Measured on TAMBO at Colca:
      walking to 20 km instead of the 5 km distance window raised the
      reported depth 6.4× and changed the selection not at all. Read this
      depth as a lower bound. Do not simply set it large, though — at 60 km
      the same run kept only 6.0% of directions against 17.5%, so the walk
      length is a parameter to check rather than to maximise.

  Not modelled at all: tau production and escape through rock (so β, the
  energy-loss constant, does not enter these numbers — the decay length used
  here is kinematics, E/m·cτ, and carries no β), neutral-current regeneration
  (so Earth-chord suppression is overstated), shower simulation, detector
  response and trigger, and any geology beyond one standard rock density.
  Nothing here has been checked against an external simulation.
